If your business is not showing on Google Maps, the cause is usually one of a short list: the profile is unverified or suspended, the primary category is wrong, a duplicate listing is splitting signals, your details are inconsistent across the web, the website is too thin, or you are checking from the wrong place. Diagnose which applies first.

Not showing up on Google Maps feels like one problem, but it is at least nine different ones with different fixes. Diagnosing the right cause first saves months. Here is how I work through it, starting with the quickest checks.

## Are you checking from the wrong place?

Map Pack results change street by street. Searching from your office and seeing yourself ranked first tells you almost nothing, because distance to the searcher is a ranking factor you cannot control. The reverse is also true: searching from home and not seeing yourself does not mean you are invisible everywhere.

Use a geo-grid tool that samples results across your whole service area instead of a single point. Almost every business that believes it ranks well is looking at one favourable pin. That grid view is also how we tracked [5x more service areas reaching the Top 3](/case-studies/map-pack-service-areas) on one home service account.

## The profile is unverified or suspended

Unverified profiles do not rank. Verification can stall if Google asks for live video verification and it is never completed. Suspended profiles vanish entirely, often without an obvious notification. Check the status in your dashboard before assuming a ranking problem.

Common suspension triggers: a virtual office address, a keyword-stuffed business name, or a service-area business displaying a street address it does not operate from. Reinstatement means fixing the cause first, then submitting a request with proof such as a trade licence, signage photos and utility bills.

## Your primary category is wrong

Covered in the [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), but worth repeating because it is the most common silent killer. A wrong primary category means you are invisible for the searches that matter, no matter how strong everything else is. A dental practice set to "Medical clinic" will struggle to appear for "dentist near me".

## Inconsistent NAP across the web

Name, address and phone number appearing differently across directories weakens Google's confidence that those listings describe the same business. That uncertainty costs you prominence.

Audit your citations, pick one canonical format, and fix the largest directories first. The process is laid out in my [local citations and NAP consistency guide](/blog/local-citations-nap-consistency-guide).

## The website underneath is too weak

Maps ranking is not decided by the profile alone. Google reads the linked website for topical relevance and trust. A one-page site with no service or location pages gives it very little to work with. Slow mobile pages, blocked crawling or pages missing from Google's index make it worse.

This is where Maps work and website SEO stop being separate projects. If the site has indexing or speed problems, [technical SEO fixes](/technical-seo) come before content.

## You are new, and prominence takes time

Prominence builds from reviews, citations, links and engagement. A profile created last month competing against one that has been active for eight years is not losing on optimization; it is losing on history. That gap closes, but not in a fortnight. For realistic expectations, see [how long Local SEO takes to work](/blog/how-long-local-seo-takes-bangladesh).

## Duplicate listings are splitting signals

Businesses that have moved, rebranded, or been listed by a previous agency often have two or three profiles competing with each other. Google splits reviews and signals between them, and sometimes ranks the wrong one.

Search your business name, your old name and your phone number in Maps directly. Claim or merge anything you find. This alone has recovered rankings for businesses that assumed they had a content problem.

## Google is filtering you out

When several similar businesses share an address or building (common in Dhaka plazas and medical centres), Google often shows one and filters the rest for the same search. Zoom into the map or open the Local Finder: if you appear there but not in the 3-pack, filtering is a likely cause. Stronger relevance and prominence for your own listing is the fix, not a fake suite number.

## Your service area is drawn wrong

Service-area businesses list up to 20 areas. Too wide and you spread relevance across places you will never realistically serve. Too narrow and you disappear from areas you want. Remember that the service area does not override distance: Google still measures from your verified location.

Define it honestly around where you actually take jobs. Twenty genuine neighbourhoods beats a hundred aspirational ones.

## A competitor is filing edits against you

Anyone can suggest an edit to your profile, and Google sometimes applies it without asking. Hours changed, category swapped, marked permanently closed. It happens more than most owners realise, and occasionally it is deliberate.

Check your profile monthly. If something changed that you did not change, revert it and report it.

Work through these in order rather than fixing everything at once, or you will never know what moved.
