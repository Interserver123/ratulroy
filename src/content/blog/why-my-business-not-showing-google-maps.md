Not showing up on Google Maps feels like one problem, but it is at least seven different ones with different fixes. Diagnosing the right cause first saves months. Here is how I work through it.

## You are checking from the wrong place

Map Pack results change street by street. Searching from your office and seeing yourself ranked first tells you almost nothing, because proximity to the searcher is a ranking factor you cannot control.

Use a geo-grid tool that samples results across your whole service area instead of a single point. Almost every business that believes it ranks well is looking at one favourable pin.

## The profile is unverified or suspended

Unverified profiles do not rank. Suspended ones vanish entirely, often without an obvious notification. Check the status in your dashboard before assuming a ranking problem.

Common suspension triggers: a virtual office address, a keyword-stuffed business name, or a service-area business displaying a street address it does not operate from.

## Your category is wrong

Covered in the [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), but worth repeating because it is the most common silent killer. Wrong primary category means you are invisible for the searches that matter, no matter how strong everything else is.

## Inconsistent NAP across the web

Name, address and phone number appearing differently across directories weakens Google's confidence that those listings describe the same business. That uncertainty costs you prominence.

Audit your citations, pick one canonical format, and fix the largest directories first.

## The website underneath is too weak

Maps ranking is not decided by the profile alone. Google reads the linked website for topical relevance and trust. A one-page site with no service or location pages gives it very little to work with.

This is where Maps work and website SEO stop being separate projects.

## You are new, and prominence takes time

Prominence accumulates from reviews, citations, links and engagement. A profile created last month competing against one that has been active for eight years is not losing on optimization; it is losing on history. That gap closes, but not in a fortnight.

## Duplicate listings are splitting signals

Businesses that have moved, rebranded, or been listed by a previous agency often have two or three profiles competing with each other. Google splits reviews and signals between them, and sometimes ranks the wrong one.

Search your business name, your old name, and your phone number in Maps directly. Claim or merge anything you find. This alone has recovered rankings for businesses that assumed they had a content problem.

## Your service area is drawn wrong

Service-area businesses set a radius or a list of areas. Too wide and Google dilutes your relevance across places you will never realistically serve. Too narrow and you disappear from areas you want.

Define it honestly around where you actually take jobs. Twenty genuine suburbs beats a hundred aspirational ones.

## A competitor is filing edits against you

Anyone can suggest an edit to your profile, and Google sometimes applies it without asking. Hours changed, category swapped, marked permanently closed. It happens more than most owners realise, and occasionally it is deliberate.

Check your profile monthly. If something changed that you did not change, revert it and report it.
