A growing share of local research now happens inside an AI assistant. The user asks for a good electrician nearby and gets a short list with reasoning, not ten blue links. Being absent from that list is a new kind of invisibility, and it is not fixed by the same tactics as classic rankings.

## AI answers compress the results page

A traditional results page shows ten options and lets the user choose. An assistant usually names two or three and explains why. The long tail of positions four to ten effectively disappears.

That raises the stakes on being clearly, unambiguously described as what you are, where you are, and who you serve.

## Entities matter more than keywords

Assistants reason over entities: a business, its location, its services, its relationships. They are not counting keyword density. If your site never states plainly what you do and where, there is nothing solid to reason about.

Structured data helps here, because it states those facts in a machine-readable form rather than leaving them to be inferred from marketing copy.

## Get cited, not just crawled

Assistants tend to cite sources that answer a question directly and completely. Pages that bury the answer under three paragraphs of preamble get skipped in favour of ones that lead with it.

Write the answer first, then the supporting detail. It suits human readers too.

- State the answer in the first sentence of a section
- Use headings that match real questions
- Keep facts consistent across your site, profile and listings

## This does not replace Local SEO

Assistants draw heavily on the same underlying signals: your profile, your reviews, your citations, your site. A business invisible in Maps is usually invisible to the assistant too.

GEO is a layer on top of solid fundamentals, not a substitute. The [generative engine optimization service](/generative-engine-optimization) assumes the Local SEO groundwork is in place.

## Where assistants get local information

They are not inventing recommendations. They draw on indexed web content, mapping data, review platforms, and licensed sources. A business with a thin site, few reviews, and inconsistent listings simply provides less material to draw on.

This is why GEO is not a separate discipline bolted onto SEO. The inputs overlap almost entirely; the presentation layer is what changed.

## Answer one question completely

Assistants favour sources that resolve a question without needing three more pages. A page covering one topic thoroughly outperforms five pages each covering a fragment of it.

This runs against the old habit of splitting content into thin keyword-targeted pages. That tactic was already declining in classic search; in AI answers it fails outright.

- One page per genuine question, not per keyword variation
- Lead each section with the answer, then the detail
- Include the specifics assistants need: areas served, hours, what you actually do

## Consistency across every surface

If your site says you serve five districts, your profile says three, and a directory says something else, an assistant has no reliable fact to state. It will usually name a competitor whose information agrees with itself.

This is the least glamorous work in AI visibility and the most commonly skipped.

## How to check whether it is working

Ask the assistants directly. Pose the questions a customer would ask about your service and area, then note whether you appear, which competitors do, and what the answer says about you.

Do it monthly and record the results. There is no rank tracker for this yet, so a manual log is genuinely the best measurement available.
