Citations are one of the most oversold services in local SEO. Bulk packages promise hundreds of listings, most on sites nobody visits. The underlying signal is real, but the way it is usually sold is not. Here is the honest version.

## What a citation actually is

A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website. It does not need to link to you. Google uses these mentions to corroborate that your business exists, where it operates, and how to reach it.

The value is corroboration, not volume. Ten consistent listings on sites Google trusts beat three hundred on directories it ignores.

## Why consistency beats count

If one directory lists "Suite 4" and another lists "Unit 4", and a third has an old phone number, Google has to decide which is correct. That uncertainty reduces confidence in your listing, and confidence feeds prominence.

Pick one exact format, down to punctuation, and use it everywhere. Then match it to your Google Business Profile exactly.

## How to audit without buying tools

Search your business name in quotes, then your phone number in quotes. Work through the results and record every variation you find. It is tedious and it works.

Fix the largest and most-trusted sources first: the major directories, your industry bodies, and anything already ranking on page one for your brand name.

- Correct the big directories before chasing obscure ones
- Claim listings where possible rather than submitting duplicates
- Remove or merge genuine duplicates of your own listing

## When citations stop helping

Once your core listings are consistent, additional citations produce sharply diminishing returns. At that point your effort is better spent on reviews, content and links.

Anyone selling you a monthly citation package after that point is selling activity, not results.

## Which directories are worth the effort

The list is shorter than most agencies imply. Your Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Facebook, and the two or three directories that genuinely rank for your industry in your country. That covers the overwhelming majority of the value.

Beyond that you are into sites with no traffic, no editorial standards, and often no real users. Google knows which is which.

- Data aggregators and the major mapping platforms first
- Industry bodies and trade associations you actually belong to
- Local chambers or council directories with real editorial oversight

## Structured data reinforces citations

Citations describe you elsewhere. LocalBusiness schema states the same facts in machine-readable form on the property you control. When the two agree, confidence rises.

The name, address and phone in your schema must match your profile exactly. A mismatch there undermines the consistency you spent effort building.

## Moving premises without losing rankings

A move is the single most disruptive event for local rankings, because every citation you built now points at the wrong place. Handled badly it costs months.

Update the profile first, then the site, then the major directories, then the long tail. Reverse that order and you create a window where the web disagrees with Google about where you are.

Clean up what exists, standardise the format, then move on. Citations are a foundation, not a growth lever. The growth comes from the wider [Local SEO system](/local-seo-service).
