An SEO specialist improves how often a business appears in Google search and Google Maps for the searches that bring customers. The work runs in a cycle: audit the website and Google Business Profile, fix technical problems, build relevant pages, strengthen reviews and citations, then measure calls and enquiries. For local businesses, the profile and Map Pack come first.

Business owners rarely get a straight answer about what SEO work consists of. Partly because it varies, and partly because vagueness protects margins. This is the honest version: what the work is, roughly in the order it happens. It is written from the Local SEO side, because that is what I do; if the term is new to you, start with [what Local SEO is](/blog/what-is-local-seo-bangladesh).

## Weeks one and two: the diagnosis

Nothing gets fixed before the diagnosis. That means auditing the Google Business Profile field by field, checking the website for technical faults (crawling, indexing, mobile speed, Core Web Vitals), mapping which keywords you already rank for in Google Search Console, and running a geo-grid to see your visibility across the whole service area rather than from one address.

Keyword research happens here too: what customers actually type, including "near me", "in [area]", "cost" and "emergency" variations, and in Bangladesh the Bangla and Banglish versions.

Most audits surface the same handful of problems: wrong primary category, incomplete profile fields, inconsistent business details across the web, and a website with no pages targeting the services people actually search for.

## The Google Business Profile work

For a local business this is the part with the most impact, and the part most often left half-finished. Categories, services with written descriptions, products, attributes, opening hours, holiday hours, photos, posts and service areas.

It is unglamorous and it moves rankings. The full field-by-field list is in the [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

- Primary category correct, additional categories genuine
- Every service listed with a description in customer language
- Real photos of the business, not stock
- Hours and special hours accurate, suggested edits checked monthly

## The website work

Maps rankings are not decided by the profile alone. Google reads the linked website to judge relevance and trust, so a one-page site gives it very little to work with.

That means service pages that go into genuine depth, area pages where you truly operate, title tags and H1s that say what each page is about, internal links that connect them, LocalBusiness schema stating the business facts in machine-readable form, and fixing whatever technical faults the audit found. That last part is covered in detail on my [technical SEO service page](/technical-seo).

## Off-site: citations, reviews, mentions

Your business details need to agree across the directories Google trusts. Inconsistency there reduces its confidence in your listing, and confidence feeds prominence. The audit method is in my [citations and NAP consistency guide](/blog/local-citations-nap-consistency-guide).

Reviews are the other half. Not buying them, which is both against policy and obvious, but building a repeatable habit of asking. Steady beats sudden. Local links and mentions (suppliers, associations, local news) round it out.

## Ongoing: measure, adjust, repeat

After the foundations, the work becomes a loop. Check the geo-grid for weak zones, look at which searches are surfacing the profile, see which pages produce calls, and put effort where the data points. That loop is how one home service client reached [+38% Map Pack visibility across its service areas](/case-studies/map-pack-service-areas).

The reporting should be readable in two minutes and tie rankings to calls, direction requests and form leads from GBP Performance and GA4. If it takes an analyst to interpret, it is not reporting; it is decoration.

## What a specialist should not do

Buying links. Buying reviews. Spinning fifty near-identical location pages with the town name swapped. Stuffing keywords into the business name. Promising a number one position. Any of these will produce a short-term chart and a long-term problem.

If someone offers guaranteed rankings, they either misunderstand how Google ranks results or are counting on you not checking. Google says plainly that nobody can pay for a better local ranking. None of this is secret. It is just laborious, and doing it consistently is what separates results from activity.
