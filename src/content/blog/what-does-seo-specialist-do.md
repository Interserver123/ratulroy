Business owners rarely get a straight answer about what SEO work consists of. Partly because it varies, and partly because vagueness protects margins. This is the honest version: what the work is, roughly in the order it happens.

## Weeks one and two: the diagnosis

Nothing gets fixed before the diagnosis. That means auditing the Google Business Profile field by field, checking the website for technical faults, mapping which keywords you already rank for, and running a geo-grid to see your visibility across the whole service area rather than from one address.

Most audits surface the same handful of problems: wrong primary category, incomplete profile fields, inconsistent business details across the web, and a website with no pages targeting the services people actually search for.

## The Google Business Profile work

This is the highest-leverage part for a local business and the part most often left half-finished. Categories, services with written descriptions, products, attributes, opening hours, holiday hours, photos, posts, and the Q&A section.

It is unglamorous and it moves rankings. The full field-by-field list is in the [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

- Primary category correct, secondary categories genuine
- Every service listed with a description in customer language
- Photos uploaded from the business, not stock
- Q&A seeded with real questions and answered

## The website work

Maps rankings are not decided by the profile alone. Google reads the linked website to judge relevance and trust, so a one-page site gives it very little to work with.

That means service pages that go into genuine depth, area pages where you truly operate, internal links that connect them, structured data stating the business facts in machine-readable form, and fixing whatever technical faults the audit found. That last part is covered in detail on the [technical SEO service page](/technical-seo).

## Off-site: citations, reviews, mentions

Your business details need to agree across the directories Google trusts. Inconsistency there reduces its confidence in your listing, and confidence feeds prominence.

Reviews are the other half. Not buying them, which is both against policy and obvious, but building a repeatable habit of asking. Steady beats sudden.

## Ongoing: measure, adjust, repeat

After the foundations, the work becomes a loop. Check the geo-grid for weak zones, look at which searches are surfacing the profile, see which pages produce calls, and put effort where the data points.

The reporting should be readable in two minutes and tie rankings to calls. If it takes an analyst to interpret, it is not reporting; it is decoration.

## What a specialist should not do

Buying links. Buying reviews. Spinning fifty near-identical location pages with the town name swapped. Promising a number one position. Any of these will produce a short-term chart and a long-term problem.

If someone offers guaranteed rankings, they are either misunderstanding how the auction works or counting on you not checking.
