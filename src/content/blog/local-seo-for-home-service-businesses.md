Local SEO for home service businesses means getting plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofing, cleaning and pest control companies into the Google Maps 3-pack and local results across their whole service area, for urgent and planned searches alike. The essentials are the right primary category, honest service-area pages, steady reviews and call tracking tied to booked jobs.

Home service searches carry the highest intent in local search. Nobody googles "emergency plumber near me" out of curiosity. The businesses that win are not the ones with the prettiest sites; they are the ones structured to be found across an entire service area at the moment of need. For the full picture of how I approach trades, see [SEO for home service companies](/seo-for-home-services).

## Emergency intent behaves differently

Urgent searches ("24-hour electrician", "AC not cooling", "blocked drain open now") convert on the first result that answers instantly. Phone number visible, hours clear, service area obvious. A beautiful site that buries the number below the fold loses to a plainer competitor that does not.

Check your own site on a phone, at arm's length, on mobile data, and count the seconds before the number is tappable.

## Set up the profile as a service-area business

Most trades do not receive customers at their premises, so the Google Business Profile should be set up as a service-area business: address hidden, up to 20 real areas listed. Choose the primary category that matches your main revenue (for example "Plumber", "Electrician", "HVAC contractor" or "Roofing contractor") and add others only for services you genuinely provide.

Then fill the services list in customer language (AC servicing, gas refill, pipe leak repair, roof leak repair) and keep hours accurate, including whether you take emergency calls. My [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers every field.

## Service-area pages, done honestly

You cannot rank across a whole region from a single homepage. You need pages that genuinely address each area you serve, with real detail about the work you do there.

The dishonest version of this is spinning fifty near-identical pages with the town name swapped. Google has recognised that pattern for years and treats it as a doorway. Build fewer pages with real substance instead.

- One page per area you actively serve, not per postcode you could reach
- Genuine local detail: jobs done, response times, landmarks you actually cover
- Distinct copy, not a template with the name swapped
- LocalBusiness schema with the right subtype (Plumber, Electrician, HVACBusiness, RoofingContractor) and `areaServed`

## Trade-specific relevance beats generic SEO

An electrician and a roofer need different content architectures because their customers search differently. Roofing is seasonal, storm-driven and high-consideration. Electrical splits between emergencies and planned work. HVAC swings between installation, servicing and summer breakdowns.

That is why I build the trade pages separately rather than as one generic service. See how it plays out for [HVAC companies](/industries/hvac-seo), [plumbing businesses](/industries/plumbing-seo) and [roofing contractors](/industries/roofing-seo).

## Track calls, not rankings

Position is a proxy. Booked jobs are the outcome. Use call tracking so you can tell which pages and which areas actually produce revenue, then invest where it is working. Pair it with the calls and direction requests in your GBP Performance report and a geo-grid scan of each service area.

Plenty of businesses rank well in places that never convert, and poorly in the two neighbourhoods that pay the bills. On one home service account, mapping that by area led to [+38% Map Pack visibility and +27% more direction requests](/case-studies/map-pack-service-areas).

## Seasonality is a content calendar

Every trade has a demand curve. AC repair and servicing spike before summer, roofing after storms and the monsoon, drainage in heavy rain, pest control in the wet season. Rankings take weeks to build, so content published when demand arrives is already too late.

Work backwards. Publish and strengthen seasonal pages six to eight weeks ahead, so they are ranking when the searches start.

- Map your demand curve from last year's job records, not guesswork
- Strengthen seasonal pages before the season, not during it
- Keep them live year round; rebuilding rank every season is wasted effort

## Reviews that mention the job and place

A review saying "great service" helps less than one saying "they fixed our AC in Mirpur the same day." The second contains the service and the location in the customer's own words, which is exactly the association you want reinforced.

You cannot script reviews and should not try, and offering discounts for them breaks Google's policies. But asking a specific question (what did we do, and where?) tends to produce specific answers.

## Response speed multiplies conversions

Ranking first is worth little if the phone rings out. For urgent trades, the business that answers wins the job regardless of who ranked above them.

Before spending on visibility, check what happens to a call at 7pm on a Saturday. Fixing that often returns more than any ranking gain.

Get the profile, service-area structure and call tracking right and home services compounds faster than almost any other local vertical.
