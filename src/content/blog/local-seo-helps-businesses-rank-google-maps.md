Local SEO helps a business rank higher on Google Maps by strengthening the three things Google uses to order local results: relevance, distance and prominence. In practice that means a complete Google Business Profile, a website that confirms what the profile claims, consistent citations and a steady flow of genuine reviews.

If you run a service business, Google Maps is usually the largest source of high-intent calls and visits. Yet most owners only ever see the top three results (the Map Pack) and never understand why their competitors keep showing up there instead of them. This guide covers how Local SEO works, what Google measures, and the system I use with clients to climb the Map Pack and stay there. ## What Local SEO actually is

Local SEO is the practice of optimizing your Google Business Profile, your website and your off-site signals so that Google treats your business as the most relevant, most trustworthy and most conveniently located option for searches in a specific area. That includes "near me" searches, "in [city]" searches like "dentist in Dhanmondi", and implicit ones where Google simply knows the searcher wants something close.

Unlike traditional SEO, which competes on a national or global keyword, Local SEO competes inside a radius. Two businesses with identical websites can rank completely differently three blocks apart, because proximity, category match and engagement change at every point on the map. The [step-by-step explanation of how Local SEO works](/blog/how-local-seo-works-bangladesh) goes deeper on that mechanism.

## What are Google's three local ranking factors?

Google's own Business Profile help documentation names three factors that decide Local Pack and Maps rankings. Every tactic in this article feeds one of them:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile and website match what the searcher typed. Driven by primary category, additional categories, services, on-page content and reviews that mention the service.
- **Distance:** how far your business location is from the searcher, or from the place named in the search. You cannot fake this, but you can win nearby areas with stronger relevance and prominence.
- **Prominence:** how well known and trusted Google thinks you are. Built from reviews and ratings, citations, backlinks, press and brand mentions, and how people interact with your listing.

## Google Business Profile: the foundation

Your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the most important asset in Local SEO. It is the listing Google ranks; your website supports it. A weak profile cannot be rescued by a great website, but a strong profile can rank even when the website is mediocre.

The non-negotiables I check on every audit:

- Primary category set to the most specific accurate option. "Dentist" or "Air conditioning repair service" beats a broad label like "Contractor" when it describes the business.
- Additional categories added only where they describe real services.
- A complete services list, each with a description written in the words customers use.
- NAP (name, address, phone) that exactly matches the website and major citations: no abbreviations, no stuffed keywords in the name.
- Real photos added regularly: job sites, team, storefront, vehicles, before and after.
- Google Posts published every 7 to 14 days with a clear call to action.
- Regular and special hours kept accurate, so you show for "open now" searches.

## Reviews: the prominence multiplier

Reviews are the fastest lever you have for prominence. Google looks at quantity, rating, recency and what reviews say. A steady stream of recent reviews that naturally mention the job and the area outperforms a one-time burst of generic 5-stars.

Build a simple system: a short follow-up message after every completed job with a direct review link or QR code, then reply to every review, good or bad, within 48 hours. Mention the job naturally in replies; don't stuff keywords into them. Never offer discounts or gifts for reviews and never filter who gets asked. Google's policies prohibit both.

## Citations and NAP consistency

Citations are mentions of your business on directories, industry sites and data aggregators. They no longer move rankings the way they did a decade ago, but inconsistent NAP across them still erodes trust. The goal is not "get 200 citations"; it is "make sure the ones that exist are clean and identical."

Prioritise the platforms that matter: Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps), Bing Places for Business, Facebook, and your top 3 to 5 industry or country directories. Fix duplicates first, then expand. ## Website SEO that supports the Map Pack

Your website is where Google checks the claims your profile is making. If your profile says you are a roofing contractor in Chattogram, your site needs to prove it with a real service page, a clear location signal and real content depth.

The pages that matter most for local rankings:

- A dedicated service page for every main service, not a single "Services" page listing them all.
- A location or service-area page for each city or neighbourhood you target, with unique content, not spun copy.
- LocalBusiness schema (or the right subtype, such as Dentist or Plumber) on the homepage and Service schema on service pages, with `sameAs` pointing to your profiles.
- An embedded Google Map and your full NAP on the contact page and footer.
- Internal links from blog content and service pages to the relevant location page, using natural anchor text.
- Fast, mobile-friendly pages. Most local searches happen on a phone, often on mobile data.

## Engagement signals: the modern tiebreaker

Once your profile and site are sound, what people do with your listing starts to matter. Calls, website clicks, direction requests, photo views and bookings all show Google that searchers find you useful. Two listings with similar fundamentals often diverge on engagement.

Practical lever: every photo, post and service description should be written to earn the click or the call. Boring profiles get ignored, and ignored profiles slide down the pack.

## How do you track Google Maps rankings properly?

Average position in a tool like Ahrefs tells you little about local performance, because rankings change at every point on the map. Use a geo-grid tracker (Local Falcon, BrightLocal or GMB Crush) to map visibility across a 7x7 or 9x9 grid centred on your service area. On one home service account, that grid view is how we lifted [Map Pack visibility by +38% and got 5x more areas into the Top 3](/case-studies/map-pack-service-areas).

Then watch the GBP Performance metrics that correlate with revenue: calls, direction requests, website clicks and bookings. Rankings are a leading indicator; these are the result. For one client, profile work produced [461 calls from the Business Profile between April and August 2024](/case-studies/gbp-calls-growth).

## The 90-day Local SEO system

- **Days 1–15:** full audit of the profile, citations, on-page SEO, schema, reviews and competitors, plus a baseline geo-grid scan.
- **Days 16–45:** foundation fixes: category and services rewrite, NAP cleanup, schema deployment, service and location page rebuilds.
- **Days 46–75:** prominence push: a review request routine, weekly Google Posts, regular real photos, and targeted local link building.
- **Days 76–90:** measure, double down on what moved the grid, and lock in the weekly routine.
