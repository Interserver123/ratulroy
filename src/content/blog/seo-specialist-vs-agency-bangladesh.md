This comparison is usually written by one side to attack the other. I run as an independent specialist, so read this knowing that, and note where I say an agency is the better choice, because sometimes it plainly is.

## Where an agency genuinely wins

If you need SEO, paid ads, social media, design, and content produced simultaneously, an agency coordinates that under one roof. Managing five freelancers yourself is a job.

Agencies also survive holidays and illness. One person cannot. If your business would be damaged by two weeks of no activity, that is a real risk to weigh.

- Multiple channels needed at once
- Large or multi-location operations
- Internal teams needing formal reporting structures
- Continuity matters more than specialisation

## Where a specialist wins

You talk to the person doing the work. Nothing is translated through an account manager into a brief for a junior executor, and nothing is lost in that translation.

The other advantage is focus. Someone who only does Local SEO sees the same failure patterns repeatedly across accounts. That pattern recognition is difficult to buy from a generalist team spread across six disciplines.

## The junior-executor problem

This is the specific risk with agencies, and it is worth naming plainly. The person who sells you the work is frequently not the person who does it. You are impressed in the meeting, then the account is handed to whoever has capacity.

It is not always the case, and good agencies avoid it. But it is worth asking directly: who will actually be working on my account, and how many other accounts do they hold?

## Cost is not the deciding factor

Specialists are often assumed to be cheaper. Sometimes true, sometimes not. An experienced independent may charge more per hour than an agency junior, and be better value because fewer hours are wasted.

Compare what is actually delivered per month rather than headline rates. Ask both what specifically happens in month one.

## Questions that work on both

The same questions expose weak providers regardless of structure. Who does the work. What gets measured. What happens if I leave in ninety days. Who owns the accounts. Show me a result with the source visible.

A strong answer to all five matters more than whether the answer comes from one person or twelve.
