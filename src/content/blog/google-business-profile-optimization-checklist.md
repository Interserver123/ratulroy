Google Business Profile optimization means completing and maintaining every field Google reads on your listing (category, services, hours, attributes, photos, posts and reviews) so you appear for more local searches and more searchers choose you. The primary category matters most; the rest decides how relevant, active and trustworthy your profile looks next to competitors.

A Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is not a directory listing you fill in once. It is the strongest ranking asset a local business owns, and Google reads roughly forty separate fields on it. Most profiles I audit are missing half of them. This is the checklist I work through, in order. If you want to understand why these fields matter for rankings first, read [how Local SEO helps businesses rank on Google Maps](/blog/local-seo-helps-businesses-rank-google-maps).

## Check verification, ownership and duplicates first

Before touching any field, confirm three things: the profile is verified, you (not a former agency or employee) hold the owner role, and there is only one listing for this location. Unverified profiles do not rank, and duplicates split reviews and signals between them.

- Verification complete, including live video verification if Google asked for it
- Owner access held by the business, with managers added as needed
- Search your name, old name and phone number in Google Maps for duplicates

If your listing is not appearing at all, work through [why a business does not show on Google Maps](/blog/why-my-business-not-showing-google-maps) before optimizing anything.

## Start with the primary category

Your primary category is the most important field on the whole profile. It tells Google which searches you are even eligible to appear in. Get it wrong and no amount of reviews or citations will rescue the listing.

Pick the category that describes what you are, not what you sell. A firm listed as "Contractor" when it is really an "HVAC contractor" is competing in the wrong pool entirely, and a practice set to "Medical clinic" instead of "Dentist" misses dental searches. Then add additional categories for genuine extra services, and stop. Padding the list with loosely related categories dilutes relevance rather than widening it.

## Get name, address and phone exactly right

Your business name must match your real-world name. No added city, no added service. Keyword-stuffed names are one of the most common reasons profiles get suspended.

Use a local phone number that is answered during your listed hours, and an address written in the same format as on your website footer and your main directory listings. In Bangladesh, where addresses often lean on landmarks, pick one written version (road, house, area, city) and use it everywhere. ## Fill in services and products properly

The services section is a free, structured place to tell Google exactly what you do. Each service accepts a name and a description, and those descriptions are indexed text.

Write them the way a customer would search. "Emergency drain unblocking" beats "Drainage solutions". If your service list mirrors the language on your website's service pages, the two reinforce each other.

- One entry per service you genuinely offer
- Descriptions written in customer language, not industry jargon
- Pricing left blank unless it is fixed and accurate
- Products added for things you sell or package (for example, "AC servicing package")

## Set service areas and booking links

Service-area businesses (plumbers, cleaners, movers, towing) should hide the street address and list the areas they actually serve, up to 20. Name real cities or neighbourhoods, such as Gulshan, Banani and Uttara, rather than drawing a vague region.

If you take bookings online, add the booking or appointment link so customers can act straight from the listing. Bookings then show up in your Performance data.

## Photos and posts are activity signals

Google measures engagement, and profiles that are actively maintained outperform dormant ones with identical fundamentals. Real photos of your premises, team, vehicles and finished work carry more weight with customers than stock imagery, and they get viewed and clicked.

Add new photos every few weeks. Cover the exterior (so people recognise the place on arrival), the interior, the team at work and the results.

## Reviews: velocity beats total

A steady trickle of reviews signals an operating business. Forty reviews collected in one week and none since signals a campaign. Ask every customer consistently using your review link or a QR code at the counter, reply to every review, and never buy, incentivise or filter them. Google's policies prohibit all three.

## Hours, attributes and skipped details

Hours seem trivial until you realise Google surfaces "open now" as a filter, and that closed businesses get pushed down at the exact moment a customer is searching. Special hours for public holidays (Eid, Pohela Boishakh, Victory Day) matter more than owners expect, because a wrong closure on a busy day costs a full day of calls.

Attributes are the checkbox list most people ignore: wheelchair access, on-site parking, appointment required, languages spoken. Each is a structured fact Google can match against a qualified search, and each is free.

- Set special hours before every public holiday, not after
- Fill every attribute that genuinely applies, leave the rest blank
- If you are a service-area business, hide the address rather than faking one

## The description is not a ranking field

This surprises people. The 750-character description does not directly influence ranking. Stuffing it with keywords achieves nothing except making it read badly to the humans who do see it.

Write it for conversion instead. State what you do, the areas you cover, and what makes working with you different. That is the job it actually performs.

## Google Posts: small effort, real engagement

Few businesses keep posting after the first month, which is exactly why consistent posts work: they are an activity signal most competitors will not match. Use update posts for news, offer posts for promotions with dates, and event posts for anything time-bound.

You do not need marketing copy. A finished job, a seasonal reminder, a common question answered. Anything showing the business is operating.

## Watch for suggested edits

Anyone can suggest an edit to your profile, and Google sometimes applies it without asking. Hours changed, category swapped, website removed. Check the profile monthly and revert anything you did not change.

## How to tell whether it worked

The Performance section of your profile reports calls, direction requests, website clicks, bookings and the searches that surfaced you. Those are the numbers to watch, not your position when you search your own name.

Track them monthly. A profile that is improving usually shows rising direction requests before rising rankings, because proximity-driven discovery moves first. One client's profile work turned into [461 calls from the Business Profile in April to August 2024, up 205% on the year before](/case-studies/gbp-calls-growth).

- **Calls and direction requests:** the closest proxy for revenue
- **Discovery versus direct searches:** discovery growth means new customers finding you
- **Photo views against competitors:** a rough prominence gauge
