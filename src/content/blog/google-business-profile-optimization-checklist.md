A Google Business Profile is not a directory listing you fill in once. It is the single strongest ranking asset a local business owns, and Google reads roughly forty separate fields on it. Most profiles I audit are missing half of them. This is the checklist I work through, in order.

## Start with the primary category

Your primary category is the highest-leverage field on the whole profile. It tells Google which searches you are even eligible to appear in. Get it wrong and no amount of reviews or citations will rescue the listing.

Pick the category that describes what you are, not what you sell. A firm listed as Contractor when it is really an HVAC Contractor is competing in the wrong pool entirely. Then add secondary categories for genuine additional services, and stop. Padding the list with loosely related categories dilutes relevance rather than widening it.

## Fill in services and products properly

The services section is a free, structured place to tell Google exactly what you do. Each service accepts a name and a description, and those descriptions are indexed text.

Write them the way a customer would search. Emergency drain unblocking beats Drainage Solutions. If your service list mirrors the language on your [Local SEO service page](/local-seo-service), the two reinforce each other.

- One entry per service you genuinely offer
- Descriptions written in customer language, not industry jargon
- Pricing left blank unless it is fixed and accurate

## Photos, posts and Q&A are ranking signals

Google measures engagement, and profiles that are actively maintained outperform dormant ones with identical fundamentals. Photos uploaded from the business location carry more weight than stock imagery.

Seed your own Q&A section with the questions customers actually ask, then answer them. Left empty, that space gets filled by strangers guessing.

## Reviews: velocity beats total

A steady trickle of reviews signals an operating business. Forty reviews collected in one week and none since signals a campaign. Ask consistently, respond to every one, and never buy them.

## Hours, attributes and skipped details

Hours seem trivial until you realise Google surfaces "open now" as a filter, and that closed businesses get demoted at the exact moment a customer is searching. Special hours for holidays matter more than owners expect, because a wrong closure on a busy day costs a full day of calls.

Attributes are the checkbox list most people ignore: wheelchair access, on-site parking, appointment required, languages spoken. Each is a structured fact Google can match against a qualified search, and each is free.

- Set special hours before every public holiday, not after
- Fill every attribute that genuinely applies, leave the rest blank
- If you are a service-area business, hide the address rather than faking one

## The description is not a ranking field

This surprises people. The 750-character description does not directly influence ranking. Stuffing it with keywords achieves nothing except making it read badly to the humans who do see it.

Write it for conversion instead. State what you do, the areas you cover, and what makes working with you different. That is the job it actually performs.

## Google Posts: small effort, real engagement

Posts expire after seven days for most types, which is why so few businesses keep up with them. That is also why they work: consistent posting is a genuine activity signal most competitors will not match.

You do not need marketing copy. A finished job, a seasonal reminder, a common question answered. Anything showing the business is operating.

## How to tell whether it worked

The insights panel inside your profile reports calls, direction requests, website clicks, and the searches that surfaced you. Those are the numbers to watch, not your position when you search your own name.

Track them monthly. A profile that is improving usually shows rising direction requests before rising rankings, because proximity-driven discovery moves first.

- **Calls and direction requests:** the closest proxy for revenue
- **Discovery versus direct searches:** discovery growth means new customers finding you
- **Photo views against competitors:** a rough prominence gauge
