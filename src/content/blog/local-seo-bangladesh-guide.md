More and more customers in Bangladesh find local businesses the same way: they pick up a phone, search Google or open Google Maps, and call one of the first few names they see. Local SEO is the work of making sure your business is one of those names. This guide covers the whole picture for a business operating here: how Google decides who appears, the problems specific to Bangladeshi addresses and search habits, what to fix first, and what it realistically costs and takes. Each section links to a deeper guide in this series when you want the detail.

## What Local SEO means in Bangladesh

Local SEO is the process of improving how visible your business is in searches with local intent: 'dentist in Dhanmondi', 'AC repair near me', 'best restaurant in Sylhet', 'car service Chattogram'. Those searches show a map with three businesses underneath, usually called the local pack or map pack, followed by ordinary website results. Local SEO covers both, but the map pack is where most of the calls come from.

It is different from general SEO because the competition is bounded by geography. A clinic in Uttara is not competing with every clinic in the country, only with the ones Google considers relevant and close to the person searching. That makes it one of the most achievable forms of search marketing for a small business, provided the basics are done properly. The guide [What Is Local SEO?](/blog/what-is-local-seo-bangladesh) covers the definition and the parts in more detail.

## How people in Bangladesh search

Search habits here have a few features that shape the work. Nearly all local searching happens on phones, often on mobile data, so a slow website or a profile without a working call button loses people quickly. Queries are typed in English, in Bangla and in romanised Bangla, and the same person may switch between them. Area names carry a lot of weight: people search 'Gulshan', 'Mirpur 10' or 'Agrabad' far more naturally than a postcode.

Google Maps itself is familiar to most smartphone users through ride-hailing and delivery apps, so people are used to looking at a map, checking reviews, and tapping to call or get directions. The business that makes those actions easy tends to win the customer.

- **Mobile first:** the profile and the website are judged on a phone screen, usually on mobile data
- **Mixed language:** English, Bangla and romanised Bangla queries for the same service
- **Neighbourhood names instead of postcodes:** 'dentist Dhanmondi', 'hotel near Zindabazar'
- **Action driven:** calls and direction requests matter more than website visits

## The three things Google ranks you on

Google states that local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Every tactic in Local SEO feeds one of these three.

- **Relevance:** how well your profile and website match what was searched. Categories, services, descriptions, website content and review text all contribute.
- **Distance:** how far your business is from the searcher, or from the area named in the search. You cannot change where you are, but you can make sure Google has your location right.
- **Prominence:** how well known and trusted Google thinks you are, judged from reviews, links, mentions across the web, and how people interact with your listing.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that actually appears in the map pack, so it is the first thing to get right. It is free, and a fully completed profile already puts you ahead of competitors who set up a listing years ago and never returned to it.

Verification is often the first hurdle here. Google chooses the method, and for many businesses in Bangladesh that now means recording a short video showing the signage, the premises and proof that you manage the business. Postal verification, where offered, can be slow. Plan for it rather than being surprised by it, and do not create a second listing if the first one stalls, because duplicates cause more problems than they solve.

- Business name exactly as it appears on your signage, with no added keywords or locations
- The most specific primary category available, plus secondary categories that genuinely apply
- Opening hours that reflect reality, including Friday hours and special hours for Ramadan and Eid
- Every service you offer listed, with a plain description
- Real photos of the premises, team and work, added regularly
- A phone number that is answered during the hours you list

## Addresses: the Bangladesh-specific problem

Inconsistent addresses are one of the most common reasons Bangladeshi businesses underperform on Maps. The same shop might be listed as 'House 12, Road 5, Dhanmondi', 'H-12, R-5, Dhanmondi R/A' and 'Opposite the Road 5 market' on three different sites, in two languages. People can usually work out that these are the same place. Search engines are less forgiving, and mismatched details weaken the trust Google places in your listing.

City and area spellings add another layer. Chittagong became Chattogram, Comilla became Cumilla and Barisal became Barishal in official use, yet customers still search with both spellings. Area names vary too: Mirpur 10, Mirpur-10 and Mirpur Section 10 all appear in the wild.

- Choose one address format and use it everywhere: profile, website, Facebook page and directories
- Place the map pin on the actual entrance, not the centre of the building or the road
- If there is no formal address, use the Plus Code Google Maps generates for your location
- Mention the landmark in your description and website, not inside the address field
- Service-area businesses that visit customers can hide their address and set the areas they serve instead

## Reviews: the most underused signal

Satisfied customers in Bangladesh rarely leave a Google review on their own. They will tell friends, message you on Facebook and come back, but the review only happens if you ask. That makes a simple, consistent request process one of the most valuable things you can set up.

- Send your Google review link by SMS or WhatsApp right after the job or visit
- Ask every customer, not only the ones you expect to be happy; selective asking breaks Google's rules
- Reviews written in Bangla are just as valuable as reviews in English
- Reply to every review, briefly and specifically, including the negative ones
- Never buy reviews or offer discounts in exchange for them; Google removes them and can suspend the profile

## Your website still matters

For many businesses in Bangladesh, the Facebook page is the main online presence, and it may bring in most of the enquiries today. It does little for Google Maps, though. Google uses your website to confirm what your profile claims: that you offer these services, in this area, at this address. A profile linked to a thin or missing website has less to support it.

The site does not need to be large. It needs a clear page for each main service, the same name, address and phone number as your profile, contact details that work on a phone, and fast loading on mobile data. Structured data (LocalBusiness schema) helps search engines read the key facts without guessing. ## Citations: fewer, cleaner listings

Citations are listings of your business on other websites. In Bangladesh the list that matters is short: your Facebook page, Bing Places, relevant industry or association directories (for example BASIS for software firms or e-CAB for e-commerce businesses), your local chamber of commerce if you are a member, and any directories specific to your trade. Accuracy matters much more than volume. A handful of consistent listings beats a hundred automated submissions to sites nobody visits.

Mentions in local news sites, event listings and partner websites also count toward prominence, and they tend to come from doing something worth mentioning rather than from outreach alone.

## AI search is already reading your profile

Google's AI Overviews and assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini increasingly answer local questions directly. They draw on the same sources Local SEO improves: your Business Profile, your reviews, your website and consistent mentions elsewhere. A business with clear, consistent facts is easier for these systems to recommend. ## Common mistakes

- Adding keywords or area names to the business name on the profile
- Two or three duplicate listings for the same location, often created by staff or customers
- A map pin placed on the wrong building or the wrong side of a main road
- Phone numbers that change or go unanswered, which frustrates customers and weakens trust
- Relying entirely on Facebook, with no website for Google to verify against
- Paying for bulk citation or backlink packages that produce nothing measurable
- Judging progress by one keyword checked from the office, instead of across the whole service area

## A simple order of work

If you are starting from scratch or from a neglected profile, this order avoids wasted effort. Each step makes the next one count for more.

- **Week one:** claim or verify the profile, then fix the name, category, hours, pin and phone number
- **Weeks two to four:** complete every profile field, add photos, and align the website and Facebook details
- **Month two:** clean up citations and duplicates, and start a steady review request routine
- **Month three onward:** build service and area content, earn local mentions, and track visibility across your area with a geo-grid
